Choose your data

First column: Date (YYYY-MM-DD). Other columns: daily NAV or price levels (initial value must be positive), one series per column. Share the sheet publicly without sign-in. Up to 10 input series and 10,000 rows.

Leave dates empty to use all input history. Existing S&P 500, SPX or ^GSPC columns are reused; otherwise Yahoo ^GSPC is downloaded and independently simulated.

Ready. Paste a public CSV or Google Sheet URL.

Understand the inputs

Provide unitized NAV or cash-flow-adjusted levels. Deposits and withdrawals are not inferred or removed. Large jumps and zero/negative NAV are flagged, not corrected. Return statistics are unavailable when NAV crosses zero.

Only common observed dates are compared; missing values are not forward-filled. Accounts are rebased to 10,000 normalized units. This is not a reconstruction of actual trades.

Data and interpretation

    STRATEGIES / S&P 500

    Performance at a glance

    All accounts start at 10,000 normalized units. Volatility and daily Sharpe assume trading-session observations (252/year). CAGR uses elapsed calendar time. Library ratios retain their stated sampling conventions.

    Explore normalized portfolio history

    Normalized NAV · starts at 10,000

    Summary measures · side by side

    NAV-based results use AlgoQuant. Execution, fee and position measures cannot be inferred from imported levels and are marked not applicable. Differences are the first input series minus each comparison; positive does not automatically mean better.

    How to read this report

    Returns are calculated from the supplied levels after rebasing each series to 10,000. Without cash-flow-adjusted input, changes in account funding will appear as gains or losses. No trades, fees or positions are invented. The S&P 500 price index excludes reinvested dividends; the convention of an input benchmark column is determined by its source.

    Historical or simulated results do not guarantee future performance. Saved numbers do not change when the source sheet is updated: run a new analysis to import a new snapshot.
